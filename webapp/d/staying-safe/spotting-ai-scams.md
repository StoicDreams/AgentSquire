## What scammers use AI for

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Convincing messages.** Phishing emails and texts used to give themselves away with poor spelling. AI writes them cleanly, in any language, and can make them personal using details from social media.
- **Cloned voices.** A few seconds of someone's voice, from a video or voicemail, can be enough to copy it. Scammers use this for calls that sound like a family member in trouble or a boss asking for an urgent payment.
- **Fake videos and photos.** Made-up video calls, celebrity endorsements of investments, fake product reviews, and fake images of events that never happened.
- **Fake AI tools.** Copycat apps, browser extensions, and websites that look like well-known assistants and steal your information or payment details.
- **Fake support.** Chatbots and "agents" on fake websites that pretend to be your bank, delivery company, or tech support.

</webui-page-segment>

## Warning signs

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Urgency.** "Right now", "before it's too late", "don't tell anyone".
- **Unusual payment.** Gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, or payment apps.
- **Requests for codes or passwords.** No real bank, company, or family member needs your one-time codes.
- **A familiar voice with an unfamiliar request.** Your grandson needing bail money, or your boss asking you to buy gift cards.
- **Pressure to stay on the line,** or not to hang up and call back.
- **Too good to be true.** Guaranteed investment returns, celebrity giveaways, or prizes you didn't enter for.

</webui-page-segment>

## How to protect yourself

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

1. **Hang up and call back.** If a call or message asks for money or information, contact the person or company yourself, using a number you already have.
1. **Agree on a family code word** for real emergencies, and ask for it if a call seems off.
1. **Never share one-time codes,** with anyone, for any reason.
1. **Get AI tools from official sources:** the company's own website or your device's official app store.
1. **Be careful what you post.** Long videos of your voice and details about your family make scams easier to tailor.
1. **Check before you share.** If an image or video seems shocking, look for it on trusted news sites before believing or sharing it.
1. **Talk about it.** Tell older relatives and kids how these scams work. Knowing about them is the best defense.

</webui-page-segment>

## If you think you've been scammed

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Contact your bank or card company right away** if you sent money or shared financial details. Acting quickly gives the best chance of stopping a payment.
- **Change your passwords,** starting with email and banking, and turn on two-step sign-in.
- **Report it.** In the US, report scams to the Federal Trade Commission at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). Elsewhere, look for your country's consumer protection or fraud reporting agency.
- **Warn others.** Tell family and friends, especially if the scammer used your name or voice.
- **Don't be embarrassed.** These scams fool careful, smart people. Reporting helps stop them.

</webui-page-segment>
