## What makes a good prompt?

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

An assistant only knows what you tell it. A short request like "summarize this" often gets an answer that's too long, too vague, or aimed at the wrong person. Each card asks for the four things that make the biggest difference:

1. **The task:** what you want done, in one sentence.
1. **The background:** who it's for, why you need it, and what you already know.
1. **What you want back:** a list, a short reply, a day-by-day plan, or a formula.
1. **The limits:** don't guess, say when you're unsure, and keep it short.

Once you've used a few cards, you'll find yourself writing prompts like this without them.

</webui-page-segment>

## How to use the cards

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

1. **Pick a recipe** that matches your task.
1. **Fill in the blanks.** Leave one empty if you'd rather fill it in later. It stays in [square brackets] for you to replace.
1. **Copy the prompt** with the copy button and paste it into a new chat.
1. **Keep talking.** If the first answer isn't right, say what to change, like "shorter" or "more formal". You don't have to start over.

</webui-page-segment>

## Tips from your squire

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Check before you paste.** Leave out passwords, account numbers, and anything your workplace doesn't allow you to share with an AI assistant.
- **Read what comes back.** Assistants can sound sure and still be wrong. Check names, numbers, and dates before you rely on them.
- **Save your favorites.** When a prompt works well, keep a copy in a note so you can use it again.

</webui-page-segment>
