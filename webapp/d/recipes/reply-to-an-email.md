<webui-quote theme="title">
Some emails take two minutes to answer and twenty minutes to word. Tell your assistant what you want to say and how it should sound, and it will draft the reply for you to check and send.
</webui-quote>

## What you'll need

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- The email you're answering.
- One or two sentences on what you want to say. Your answer matters more than the wording.
- A few words on how it should sound, such as "warm", "firm but polite", or "brief".

</webui-page-segment>

## How to use it

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

1. **Fill in the blanks** and paste the email you received into the last box.
1. **Copy the prompt** and paste it into a new chat with your assistant.
1. **Answer its questions.** The prompt asks it to check with you rather than make things up, so it may ask about a date or a detail first.
1. **Adjust the draft.** Ask for changes in plain words, like "shorter", "less formal", or "don't apologize".
1. **Read it once more and send it yourself,** from your own email.

</webui-page-segment>

## Tips from your squire

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Say what you want to say.** "Decline politely" works much better than "reply to this". The assistant writes the words, but the answer has to come from you.
- **Check every fact.** Dates, prices, names, and promises in the draft should be ones you made. If you see one you didn't, take it out.
- **Watch for private details.** If the email contains someone's personal or confidential information, remove it before pasting, or check your workplace's rules on AI tools first.
- **Make it sound like you.** If the draft is too polished, ask it to "write it the way I'd say it out loud", or paste one of your own emails and ask it to match the style.

</webui-page-segment>

## An example

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

Sam runs a small bakery and gets a request for 200 cupcakes by Saturday, which isn't possible. Sam fills in "Sorry, I can't do 200 by Saturday, but I can do 100, or 200 by the following Wednesday" and "friendly and helpful". The assistant drafts a short, warm reply offering both options, and Sam adds a price before sending it.
