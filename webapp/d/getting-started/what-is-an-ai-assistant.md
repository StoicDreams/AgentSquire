## Chatbots, assistants, and agents

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

These words get mixed up, so here's how I use them on this site.

- **Chatbot:** the little chat window on a company's website, like a bank or an airline. Many follow a script and only handle a few questions about that company.
- **AI assistant:** a general helper you talk to in your own words, like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot. It can write, explain, summarize, brainstorm, and answer questions on almost any topic. You ask, it answers, and you decide what to do with the answer.
- **AI agent:** an assistant that can also take actions for you, over several steps. It might search the web, read your email, fill in a form, or work through a to-do list, checking in with you along the way.

The line between assistants and agents is blurring. The big assistants can now search the web, read files, and connect to your other apps, so most of them can act as agents when you let them.

</webui-page-segment>

## What they're good at

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Writing and rewriting:** emails, letters, posts, and reports, in whatever tone you need.
- **Summarizing:** long documents, meeting notes, and email threads, cut down to what matters.
- **Explaining:** a topic, a document, or a confusing bill, at whatever level you like.
- **Brainstorming:** names, gift ideas, plans, and ways around a problem.
- **Organizing:** turning a jumble of notes into a list, a plan, or a table.
- **Spreadsheets and numbers:** writing formulas and explaining what they do.
- **Practice:** rehearsing a hard conversation, a job interview, or a new language.

The common thread: they're great at work where you can judge the result.

</webui-page-segment>

## Where they struggle

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Facts you can't check.** Assistants can be wrong while sounding sure, and can make up sources and quotes.
- **Very recent news,** unless they search the web.
- **Your private situation.** They only know what you tell them, and you shouldn't tell them everything.
- **Big decisions.** They're not a doctor, lawyer, or financial adviser.

I've written a full page on [the risks of using AI](/ai-risks), including why assistants get things wrong. It's worth a read before you rely on one for anything important.

</webui-page-segment>

## How they work, in one paragraph

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

An AI assistant is built on a "language model" that learned from an enormous amount of text. When you write to it, it works out a likely reply, word by word. That's why it can write about almost anything in almost any style, and also why it can produce something that sounds right but isn't. It doesn't think like a person, and it doesn't "know" things the way you do, but it's very good with words.

</webui-page-segment>
