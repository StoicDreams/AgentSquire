## What connecting does

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

Out of the box, an assistant only sees what you type or upload. When you connect an app, like your email, cloud storage, or calendar, you give the assistant permission to reach into that app for you. Then you can ask things like:

- "Find the email from the plumber with the quote, and summarize it."
- "What's on my calendar next Tuesday, and is there time for a two-hour meeting?"
- "Find last year's budget spreadsheet in my drive and compare it with this year's."

Some connections can only read. Others can also act: draft and send emails, create calendar events, or edit files. Assistants call these connections by different names, such as connectors, apps, connected apps, or plugins.

</webui-page-segment>

## Built in or connected

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

- **Built in:** Gemini works with Gmail, Docs, and other Google apps through your Google account, and Copilot works with Word, Outlook, and Excel through Microsoft 365. If you already live in one of those, this is the easiest way to start.
- **Connected:** ChatGPT, Claude, and the others can connect to many outside apps, including Google and Microsoft ones, once you sign in to them and grant permission. Which apps are available, and on which plans, varies and changes often.

</webui-page-segment>

## What is MCP?

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

You may see "MCP" mentioned. It stands for **Model Context Protocol**, and it's a shared standard for connecting AI assistants to other apps and services.

Think of it like a universal plug. Before standards like this, every assistant needed its own custom connection to every app. With MCP, a company can build one connector for its app, and any assistant that supports MCP can use it. Many assistants and apps now support it.

You don't need to know how it works to use it. What matters for you is the same as with any connection: what can it see, what can it do, and do you trust who made it?

</webui-page-segment>

## Connect safely

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

1. **Start with read-only, if you can.** Let it find and summarize before you let it send or change anything.
1. **Connect only what you need.** One email account is plenty to start. You don't have to connect everything.
1. **Read the permission screen.** When you sign in to connect, the app shows what the assistant will be allowed to do. If it asks for more than you expected, stop.
1. **Keep approvals on.** Choose settings that ask you before it sends, deletes, buys, or posts anything, and read what it's about to do before you say yes.
1. **Use official connectors.** Prefer the ones offered inside your assistant or by the app's own company. Be wary of connectors from sources you don't know.
1. **Watch for hidden instructions.** An email or document can contain instructions written for the AI. If the assistant suddenly suggests something strange after reading something, don't approve it. My page on [the risks of using AI](/ai-risks) explains this "prompt injection".
1. **Follow your work's rules.** Don't connect work accounts to a personal assistant unless your work allows it.
1. **Disconnect what you don't use.** Review your connected apps every few months and remove the ones you no longer need.

</webui-page-segment>

## A good first connection

<webui-page-segment elevation="10">

Try connecting your calendar or cloud storage, read-only, and ask simple questions: "What do I have on this week?" or "Find my car insurance documents." Once you're comfortable with how it finds and summarizes, decide whether you want it to do more.

</webui-page-segment>
