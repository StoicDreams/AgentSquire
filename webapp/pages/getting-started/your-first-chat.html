The first time you talk to an AI assistant, it's tempting to ask it something tricky to test it. You'll learn much more by giving it a small, real job. Here's how to set up, what to ask, and how to get a good answer.

Before you start

  1. Pick an assistant. Any of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Copilot is fine to start with. If you can't decide, take the three-question quiz. If your work or school gives you one, use that one for work.
  2. Sign up on the official website or app. Get the app from the company's own website or your phone's official app store. Copycat apps exist.
  3. Start on the free plan. You can upgrade later if you need to.
  4. Look at the privacy settings. Check whether your chats are used to train the AI, and turn that off if you prefer. My page on the risks of using AI explains why.

Start with something real

Pick a small task you'd actually do today. Here are three to try.

A reply you've been putting off:

Help me write a short, friendly reply to my neighbor. They asked if I can water their plants next week. I can do Monday to Wednesday, but not the weekend.

Something you want to understand:

Explain what an "escrow account" on a mortgage is, in plain language, as if I've never owned a home. Keep it under 150 words.

A plan:

I have chicken, rice, spinach, and a lemon. Suggest three simple dinners I can make in under 30 minutes, with a short shopping list for anything missing.

How to ask for what you want

A good request usually has four parts. You don't need all four every time, but each one helps.

  1. The task: what you want, in one sentence. "Write a reply", "Explain", "Suggest three".
  2. The background: who it's for, what it's about, and anything it can't guess. "My neighbor", "I've never owned a home".
  3. What you want back: the shape of the answer. "Short", "a list", "under 150 words", "with a shopping list".
  4. The limits: what to avoid. "Not the weekend", "no jargon", "don't guess".

Write the way you'd brief a helpful new colleague: in full sentences, with the details that matter. You don't need special words or tricks.

Keep the conversation going

The first answer is a draft, not the final word. Instead of starting over, tell it what to change:

  • "Shorter, please."
  • "Make it warmer."
  • "I don't eat spinach. Swap it for something else."
  • "Explain the second point in more detail."
  • "Why did you suggest that?"

It remembers everything said earlier in the same chat, so you can keep refining. When you switch to a different topic, start a new chat, so earlier details don't get mixed in.

Check its work

  • Read the whole answer before you use it.
  • Check names, numbers, dates, and anything you'll rely on.
  • If something seems off, ask "Are you sure?" or "Where does that come from?", and check it yourself if it matters.

Set it up for next time

Once you've had a few chats, tell your assistant about yourself so it doesn't need reminding. The Instructions Builder writes custom instructions for you to paste into its settings. For everyday tasks, the Prompt Cards give you fill-in-the-blank prompts to copy.
