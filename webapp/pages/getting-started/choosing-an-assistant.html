The big four assistants are more alike than their makers would like you to think. Any of them can help you write, plan, and learn. The best one for you usually comes down to where you work, which apps you use, and which one you enjoy talking to.

The short answer

  1. If your work or school gives you an assistant, use it for work. It's the one they've approved for work information.
  2. If you live in Google's apps, like Gmail and Docs, start with Gemini.
  3. If you live in Microsoft's apps, like Outlook and Word with Microsoft 365, start with Copilot.
  4. Otherwise, try ChatGPT and Claude side by side.

Whichever you pick, try two on their free plans with the same real task for a week, and keep the one you like working with. The Which Assistant Fits Me? quiz walks you through this in three questions.

All four can write, answer questions, search the web, read files you give them, and talk out loud in their phone apps.

Each one in a few words

  • ChatGPT is the most widely used assistant, so help, guides, and how-to videos are easy to find. A good all-rounder if you don't have a strong reason to pick another.
  • Claude is known for clear, natural writing and for working through long documents. A good fit if you mostly write, edit, or read long material. It doesn't make pictures.
  • Gemini is Google's assistant. A good fit if your email, documents, and calendar are already in Google, because it can help right inside them.
  • Microsoft Copilot is Microsoft's assistant. A good fit if you use Word, Excel, and Outlook with Microsoft 365, because it can help right inside them, and you may already be paying for it.

What it costs

As of November 2026, all four have a free plan with daily limits, and their main paid plans cost about $20 a month in the US, or about $10 for Microsoft 365 Personal, which includes Copilot. A Google AI plan can be shared with your family. The AI Plan Cost Calculator works out the cost for you and your household.

Other things worth checking

  • Privacy settings: each one handles your chats differently. Check whether your chats are used for training, and whether you can turn that off.
  • Your devices: make sure it has an app for the phone and computer you use.
  • How it feels: you'll use it a lot, so notice which one's answers you find easiest to work with. This matters more than any comparison.

These assistants change quickly. What each one offers was checked in November 2026, and I'll keep this page up to date.
