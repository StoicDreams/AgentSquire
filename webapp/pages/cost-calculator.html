How the calculator picks a plan

  • A few times a week: the free plan. Every assistant has one, with daily limits.
  • Most days: each company's main paid plan, the one most people upgrade to.
  • All day, for big jobs: the plan with much higher limits, for long documents, lots of research, or using it for hours.

Some companies also sell a cheaper plan between free and the main one, such as ChatGPT Go or Google AI Plus. They can be a good step up if the free plan runs out but you don't need everything the main plan offers.

Sharing with your household

  • Google lets one Google AI plan be shared with up to five others in your Google family group, so a household can pay for one plan.
  • Microsoft 365 Family covers up to six people for Word, Excel, and storage, but only the owner gets Copilot. Everyone who wants Copilot needs their own plan.
  • ChatGPT and Claude plans are for one person each.

Buying for a team at work? Business plans are priced per person and add controls for managing who has access. Ask your IT team first, since your work may already have one.

Tips from your squire

  • Start free. Use the free plan for a couple of weeks before paying. You'll learn whether you hit its limits.
  • Count what you already pay for. If you already pay for Microsoft 365, you may already have Copilot. Check before buying a second assistant.
  • Look for trials and first-month deals, and set a reminder before the full price starts.
  • Pay yearly only once you're sure. Yearly plans save money, but you pay up front.
  • Not sure which assistant to choose? Try the Which Assistant Fits Me? quiz.
