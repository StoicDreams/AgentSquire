Planning a trip means a lot of tabs and a lot of choices. Tell your assistant where you're going, who's coming, and what you enjoy, and it will sketch a day-by-day plan you can shape into your own.

What you'll need

  • Where you're going, and roughly when and for how long.
  • Who's going, including ages if children are coming.
  • A few things you enjoy, and a few you'd rather skip.
  • A rough budget, even just "tight", "moderate", or "comfortable".

Your prompt

Everything here runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere until you paste it into your assistant yourself.

How to use it

  1. Fill in the blanks. The more you say about what you enjoy, the better the plan fits you.
  2. Copy the prompt and paste it into a new chat with your assistant.
  3. Shape the plan. Ask for changes, like "less walking on day two", "a cheaper lunch spot", or "swap the museum for something outdoors".
  4. Confirm the details yourself on official websites before you book anything.

Tips from your squire

  • Treat it as a first draft. Assistants can suggest a place that has closed, moved, or changed its hours. The prompt asks it to say what to double-check, so check those things.
  • Say what you don't want. "No early mornings" or "we don't like crowds" changes a plan more than another thing you like.
  • Ask about getting around. Ask how to get between places and how long it takes, so the plan isn't packed too tightly.
  • Book through official sites. Use the assistant for ideas, then book through the venue, airline, or a booking site you trust.

An example

A family of three plans four days in Lisbon in early May. They enjoy food markets, history, and short walks, and want to keep the budget moderate. The plan that comes back puts sights that are close together on the same day, saves a rainy-day option for each afternoon, and points out the attractions that usually need tickets in advance. They swap one museum for a beach afternoon, check opening hours, and book.
